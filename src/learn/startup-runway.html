Startup runway: how many months do you really have?

Planning · 5 min read · Updated November 2026

Runway is how long your business can operate before the cash runs out. It is the clock every founder is racing, and it is easy to misread.

The basic formula

Runway (months) = cash in the bank ÷ net monthly burn, where net burn = monthly costs − monthly revenue.

With $180,000 in the bank, $27,000 of monthly costs and $9,000 of monthly revenue, net burn is $18,000 and runway is 10 months.

Gross burn vs net burn

Gross burn is everything you spend each month. Net burn subtracts revenue. Investors usually ask for net burn, but gross burn shows how exposed you are if revenue drops — a useful stress test.

Growth changes the answer

If revenue in the example grows 5% a month, burn shrinks each month and runway stretches. If revenue grows fast enough to exceed costs before the cash runs out, the company is “default alive”. The runway calculator models this month by month.

Five levers that extend runway

  1. Raise prices for new customers — often the fastest, cheapest lever.
  2. Collect faster: annual prepay discounts, deposits, shorter invoice terms.
  3. Cut spend that doesn’t drive revenue — unused software and low-return ads first.
  4. Pace hiring to revenue milestones instead of the calendar.
  5. Line up funding early: raising usually takes 3–6 months, so start with at least 9 months of runway left.

The bottom line

Recalculate runway monthly with real numbers, not plans. The founders who survive are rarely the ones with the most cash — they are the ones who knew exactly how much time they had.
