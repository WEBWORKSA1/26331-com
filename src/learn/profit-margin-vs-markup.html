Profit margin vs markup: the pricing mistake that costs owners money

Pricing · 6 min read · Updated November 2026

Ask ten small-business owners what “a 50% margin” means and several will describe a 50% markup. The two sound alike, use the same inputs and are constantly confused, but they answer different questions. Mixing them up is one of the most common reasons a business that looks profitable on paper struggles to pay its bills.

The two formulas

Markup compares profit to cost: markup = (price − cost) ÷ cost.

Margin compares profit to price: margin = (price − cost) ÷ price.

Take a product that costs $50 and sells for $75. The profit is $25. As a markup that is $25 ÷ $50 = 50%. As a margin it is $25 ÷ $75 = 33.3%. Same product, same sale, two very different percentages.

Why margin is the number that matters

Your rent, wages, software and loan payments are paid out of revenue, not out of cost. Margin tells you how many cents of every sales dollar are left to cover those expenses and produce profit. If your overheads take 30% of revenue and your margin is 33%, you are keeping only about 3 cents per dollar — even though a “50% markup” sounds comfortable.

The conversion is simple: margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup), and markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin).

How to price for the margin you need

Work backwards from the margin: price = cost ÷ (1 − target margin). If an item costs $30 and you need a 40% margin, the price is $30 ÷ 0.60 = $50. Check it: $20 profit ÷ $50 price = 40%.

A common shortcut — adding 40% to cost — gives $42, which is only a 28.6% margin. On a thousand units that difference is $8,000 of profit you thought you had.

Three habits that protect your margin

Use landed cost. Include shipping, duties, payment fees and packaging in “cost”. A 3% card fee alone moves a 40% margin to 37%.

Know your discount ceiling. A 15% discount on a 40% margin product needs roughly 60% more unit sales to earn the same gross profit. Run it through the discount impact calculator before your next sale.

Review quarterly. Supplier prices drift. A margin set once and never revisited quietly shrinks.

The bottom line

Quote markup to your suppliers if you like, but run your business on margin. It is the number that connects each sale to whether you can pay your bills.
