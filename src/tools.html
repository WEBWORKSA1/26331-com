How to use these calculators well

Every calculator here uses standard, published formulas: amortized payments for loans and mortgages, monthly compounding for savings, contribution margin for break-even, and the familiar lifetime-value formula (average revenue × gross margin ÷ churn) for subscription businesses. Results are rounded for display; the underlying math keeps full precision.

Three habits make the numbers more useful. First, run a pessimistic case and an optimistic case, not just your best guess. Second, change one input at a time so you can see which assumption matters most. Third, write the result down with the date — numbers you revisit monthly are the ones that change your decisions.

The currency converter uses reference rates refreshed once a day by an automated data job. They are suitable for planning, not for trading; your bank or card provider will apply its own rate and fees.
