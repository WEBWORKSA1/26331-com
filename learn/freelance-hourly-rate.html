How to price freelance work: the hourly rate formula

Business · 5 min read · Updated November 2026

Most new freelancers take their old salary, divide by 2,080 hours and quote that. It almost always leaves them underpaid, because a freelancer pays their own taxes and costs and cannot bill every working hour.

The formula

Hourly rate = (target take-home ÷ (1 − tax rate) + business costs) ÷ billable hours per year

Step-by-step example

Target take-home pay$90,000
Effective tax rate28%
Pre-tax income needed$90,000 ÷ 0.72 = $125,000
Business costs (software, insurance, equipment, accounting)$8,000
Revenue needed$133,000
Billable hours (46 weeks × 25 hours)1,150
Minimum hourly rate≈ $115.65

Note the 25 billable hours a week. Admin, sales, invoicing and learning take real time; many freelancers bill only 50–70% of the hours they work.

Run your own numbers in the freelance hourly rate calculator.

Beyond the minimum

The formula gives a floor, not a price. Your market rate depends on demand for your skill, your track record and the value you create. If a project will earn a client $50,000, a fixed fee tied to that outcome may be fairer to both sides than hours.

Three pricing habits

Quote day or project rates once you are established; it rewards efficiency instead of punishing it.

Raise rates yearly, at least with inflation, and always for new clients first.

Track effective hourly rate per client: fee ÷ actual hours. It reveals which clients are quietly unprofitable.

Tax rates vary by country and situation. This guide is educational and not tax advice.
