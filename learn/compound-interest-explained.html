Compound interest explained with real numbers

Money · 7 min read · Updated November 2026

Compound interest means earning returns on your past returns. Simple interest pays on the original amount only; compounding pays on a growing balance. Over short periods the difference is small. Over decades it dominates everything else.

The formula

Future value = principal × (1 + rate)years. With $10,000 at 7% a year for 30 years, that is 10,000 × 1.0730 ≈ $76,123. Simple interest at the same rate would give $31,000.

The Rule of 72

Divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate how many years it takes money to double. At 7%, that is about 10.3 years; at 4%, about 18; at 10%, about 7.2. It is a quick sanity check for any savings or debt figure.

Why starting early beats saving more

Suppose you invest $300 a month at a 7% annual return, compounded monthly.

StartYears investedTotal contributedApproximate value
Now30$108,000$366,000
In 10 years20$72,000$156,000

Waiting ten years cuts contributions by a third but the final value by more than half. The last decade of compounding does the heaviest lifting, so it is the decade you cannot afford to lose.

Model your own plan with the compound interest calculator or set a target with the savings goal planner.

Compounding works against you too

Credit-card debt compounds at rates often above 20%. A $6,000 balance at 22.99% APR with $250 monthly payments takes 33 months to clear and costs about $2,100 in interest. Run your balance through the credit card payoff calculator to see the real cost.

Two things that quietly shrink compounding

Inflation. A 7% return with 3% inflation is roughly a 4% real return. Plan in today’s dollars with the inflation calculator.

Fees. A 1% annual fee looks small, but over 30 years on a 7% return it can consume roughly a quarter of the final balance. Always compare costs before choosing where to invest.

The bottom line

Start as early as you can, keep costs low, avoid high-interest debt, and let time do the work. The math is simple; the discipline is the hard part.
